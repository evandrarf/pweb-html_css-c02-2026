Featured Works

Julius Robert Oppenheimer was an American theoretical physicist, widely known as the "father of the atomic bomb" for his role directing the Manhattan Project during World War II. His work reshaped nuclear physics and left a lasting mark on science, policy, and the modern world.

  • Born–Oppenheimer Approximation

    With Max Born, formulated the Born–Oppenheimer approximation that separates nuclear and electronic motion in molecules, becoming a foundation of modern quantum chemistry.

  • Quantum Tunneling & Alpha Decay

    Developed the theory of quantum tunneling, later used to explain alpha decay, one of the earliest applications of quantum mechanics to atomic nuclei.

  • Oppenheimer–Phillips Process

    With Melba Phillips, explained deuteron-induced nuclear reactions, now known as the Oppenheimer–Phillips process, important in experimental nuclear physics.

  • Cosmic Ray Showers Theory

    Developed the theory of cosmic ray showers—cascades of high-energy particles in the atmosphere—contributing to early astrophysics and particle physics.

  • Neutron Stars & Black Holes

    With George Volkoff and Hartland Snyder, predicted neutron stars and the gravitational collapse of massive stars into what are now called black holes.

  • Manhattan Project

    Directed the Los Alamos Laboratory during the Manhattan Project, leading the development of the first atomic bombs and the 1945 Trinity test.

  • Berkeley Years

    Professor of physics at UC Berkeley, building one of the leading centers of theoretical physics in the U.S. and mentoring a generation of physicists.

  • IAS & Security Controversy

    Director of the Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton; engaged in nuclear policy debates and faced a 1954 security hearing that revoked his clearance.
